🪙 1,000 Coins · Exam Prep

1 · Deep Explanation

The question: Toss each of 1,000 fair coins 10 times. What is the probability that at least one coin gets 10 heads?
Answer: 1 − (1023/1024)^1000 ≈ 1 − 0.377 = 0.6231 ≈ 62.3%
Step 1 – One coin gets 10 heads

P(heads) = 1/2 on each toss. Tosses are independent, so we multiply: (1/2)^10 = 1/1024 ≈ 0.00097 ≈ 0.001 = 0.1%. Rare for one coin.

Analogy: guessing 10 coin flips in a row correctly. One try is hopeless, but this is a lottery with 1,000 tickets.
Step 2 – No coin gets 10 heads

One coin fails with probability 1 − 1/1024 = 1023/1024. Coins are independent of each other, so multiply across all 1,000: (1023/1024)^1000 ≈ 0.377. The more coins, the more likely at least one succeeds.

Step 3 – Complement rule

"None" and "at least one" are opposites; their probabilities add to 1. So P(at least one) = 1 − (1023/1024)^1000.

Memory trigger: "At least one" → think "1 minus none".
Step 4 – Approximation with exponentials

Multiplying a number very close to 1 many times gives a smaller number. Key idea: (1 − 1/n)^n ≈ e^−1. More generally, for small x and large k: (1 − x)^k ≈ e^(−kx).

(1023/1024)^1000 = (1 − 1/1024)^1000 ≈ e^(−1000/1024) = e^(−0.9765625) ≈ e^−0.976 ≈ 0.377.

Therefore P(at least one) ≈ 1 − 0.377 = 0.6231 → about 62.3%. Rare per coin, but with 1,000 repeats it is likely to occur at least once.

Appendix A – Where −0.976 comes from

1) Write (1023/1024)^1000. 2) Use (1 − x)^k ≈ e^(−kx). 3) Here x = 1/1024, k = 1000. 4) Exponent = −1000/1024 = −0.9765625 ≈ −0.976.

Bonus link to Machine Learning

This is the "many hypotheses" effect: if you test enough candidates, a rare lucky result (10 heads) becomes likely by chance alone. So seeing one remarkable coin among many does not prove that coin is special. This is why a fit chosen from a big pool looks good on the sample but may not generalize.

Common mistakes & traps
  • Adding instead of multiplying (1000 × 1/1024 ≈ 0.977 overcounts; events overlap).
  • Using 1/1024 as the failure probability; failure is 1023/1024.
  • Forgetting the final 1 − and answering 0.377.
  • Dropping independence: it justifies every multiplication.

2 · Smart Summary

  • Single coin, 10 heads: (1/2)^10 = 1/1024 ≈ 0.00097 ≈ 0.1%
  • Single coin fails: 1023/1024
  • All 1,000 fail: (1023/1024)^1000 ≈ 0.377 (independence)
  • At least one: 1 − 0.377 = 0.6231 ≈ 62.3% (complement rule)
  • Tool: (1 − x)^k ≈ e^(−kx); here exponent −1000/1024 ≈ −0.976
  • Intuition: rare event × many trials = likely to happen
Mnemonic: "I-C-E" = Independence → multiply · Complement → 1 − none · Exponential → e^(−kx).
