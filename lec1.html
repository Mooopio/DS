2. Raw data → Information → Knowledge

Raw data = values with little or no description, e.g. “3” or “three apples”. Raw data is usually not described.

Information = data after processing or after adding description (metadata: data about data). Example: in a database, a column has a name and meaning. The more you describe, the more accurate the information.

Knowledge = understanding a pattern that is not explicitly written anywhere; you must search for it in the data.

Analogy: A receipt is information. Noticing that you overspend every payday is knowledge: no receipt says it, you discover it by looking across many receipts.

Business examples: “How do my sales change across the year?” If a table already says it, that is information. If not, you must discover it. “Which ads (TV, newspapers, internet) give the highest net profit?” The answer is not recorded; you learn it from data.

What is a pattern? An event that repeats once, twice, many times until you reach statistical confidence, meaning it is repeated enough to trust mathematically. Examples: sales drop mid-year; sales rise with one ad type.

4. Abstraction, generalization, mathematical formulation

Three linked ideas: abstraction (keep the repeating essence, drop details), generalization (apply it to new cases), mathematical formulation (write it as math).

People learn two ways: examples → concept (several solved examples, then you see the idea), or concept → examples. Changing the numbers in similar problems builds the concept in your head.

Abstraction in CS: programs are built so users do not face complexity. Many users and many conditions in one system require levels of abstraction, with a translation down to machine language (what the computer really runs, not assembly or the 0/1 you casually mention).

Course goal: make the computer learn. A program receives many detailed examples, abstracts the repeated details, and builds its own knowledge.

5. Worked example: experience vs salary

Data: 1 yr → 10, 2 yrs → 20, 3 yrs → 30. Question: 4 years? You answer 40, because you abstracted a pattern (salary = 10 × years). You made a model.

Model = a representation of reality, not reality, like an architect’s mock-up of a building. It is useful because we can work with it. ChatGPT-like systems are far bigger models, but the idea is the same.

Unseen questions: 1.5 years → 15, though no such record exists. The model answers what the database cannot (no record there). Between two points you place the answer in the middle (interpolation). The question usually concerns y (salary) given x (years). Also ask: which field? which technology? Context changes the answer.

6. The line: y = mx + c
  • m = slope = Δy/Δx (change in y between any two points ÷ change in x). It controls steepness for all points on the line.
  • c = y-intercept: where the line crosses the y-axis (x = 0). It shifts the line up/down.
  • x is the domain (inputs); y is the range (outputs).
  • Seeing a picture and expressing it as an equation is mathematical formulation. Many different lines exist; m and c pick the one that fits your points.
7. Beyond lines: curves and circles
  • Experience may start high, then fall, then rise: not a line. A quadratic (x²) gives a curve with one turn.
  • x³ controls the number of curves (up to two turns). Here we have a single variable x.
  • Circle: x² + y² = r². With r = 1: x² + y² = 1 (the unit circle).
  • Key question: what controls the shape of a graph? (coefficients and powers). Assignment: draw these equations in GeoGebra/Desmos and observe.
